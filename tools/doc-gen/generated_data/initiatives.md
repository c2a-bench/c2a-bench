# Skyway Air — 2026 Initiatives and Roadmap

## Context & Framing

### Planning horizon
This roadmap covers January 1–December 31, 2026. Milestones through October 30, 2026 reflect delivery status as of the roadmap review date; Q4 milestones remain planned unless otherwise stated.

### Driving principles and strategic goals

- Make flying simple and fair through plain-language policies, predictable outcomes, and visible customer status updates.
- Deliver on the “plans change” promise by improving disruption handling, rebooking, refunds, and frontline decision-making.
- Prioritize operationally feasible changes that can be implemented consistently across digital, contact-center, and airport channels.
- Protect regulatory compliance, financial controls, and service quality as customer policies change.
- Reduce avoidable customer effort and employee rework while maintaining Skyway’s value-carrier cost discipline.

## Initiatives

### 1. Refund speed-up

**Description and scope**  
Reduce the target time for eligible customer refunds from 21 calendar days to 7 calendar days. Scope includes refund eligibility routing, payment and reconciliation workflows, exception handling, customer communications, and performance reporting across direct and assisted sales channels.

**Owner**  
Customer Support + Finance

**Priority**  
P0

**Key milestones**

| Target date | Milestone | Status |
|---|---|---|
| March 31, 2026 | Map current refund workflows, identify delays by payment method and channel, and establish baseline refund-time and backlog metrics. | Complete |
| May 15, 2026 | Implement revised refund decision matrix, automated eligibility checks for standard cases, and daily Finance–Support exception review. | Complete |
| July 1, 2026 | Updated refund service standard takes effect: eligible refunds are issued within 7 calendar days of completed eligibility validation. | Complete |
| September 30, 2026 | Extend refund-status notifications and self-service status visibility to all direct-booking customers; retire manual queue for standard cases. | Complete |
| December 15, 2026 | Complete year-end control review, resolve remaining payment-method exceptions, and set 2027 service-level targets. | Planned |

**Dependencies and risks**

- Payment processor settlement timing and travel-agent refund processes may constrain end-to-end timing.
- Automation must preserve fraud, chargeback, and accounting controls.
- Complex itineraries, involuntary refunds, and disrupted-trip exceptions require clear escalation ownership.
- Policy and agent guidance must remain synchronized with the disruption policy refresh.

### 2. Disruption policy refresh

**Description and scope**  
Replace fragmented delay and cancellation guidance with a single, plain-language customer policy covering compensation eligibility, meal and hotel support where applicable, voucher values and expiry, rebooking choices, refunds, exceptions, and approval limits. Align policy execution across website, app, contact center, airport desks, and operational control teams.

**Owner**  
Policy and Compliance

**Priority**  
P0

**Key milestones**

| Target date | Milestone | Status |
|---|---|---|
| February 28, 2026 | Complete legal, regulatory, operational, and customer-impact review of existing disruption rules and exception patterns. | Complete |
| April 15, 2026 | Publish approved internal policy standard, decision trees, and channel-specific operating procedures. | Complete |
| June 1, 2026 | Phase 1 effective date: revised rebooking rules and plain-language delay/cancellation communications take effect. | Complete |
| August 15, 2026 | Phase 2 effective date: standardized compensation and voucher rules, including eligibility and approval limits, take effect. | Complete |
| October 31, 2026 | Audit policy adherence across Support and airport locations; address training and tooling gaps before peak holiday operations. | In progress |
| December 10, 2026 | Complete post-implementation review using disruption cases, complaints, voucher usage, and frontline feedback; approve 2027 adjustments. | Planned |

**Dependencies and risks**

- Legal and regulatory requirements vary by itinerary and disruption cause, particularly for international service.
- Airport staffing, hotel availability, and partner capacity can affect the ability to deliver published commitments during major disruptions.
- All customer-facing channels require simultaneous content updates to avoid contradictory guidance.
- Vouchers and compensation require Finance controls and clear accounting treatment.

### 3. Baggage claims online

**Description and scope**  
Enable customers to submit and track delayed- and damaged-baggage claims in the Skyway app. The capability will collect claim details and supporting evidence, provide case-status updates, connect customers to baggage service when necessary, and integrate with existing baggage tracing and claims processes.

**Owner**  
Airport and Ground Operations + Digital

**Priority**  
P1

**Key milestones**

| Target date | Milestone | Status |
|---|---|---|
| March 15, 2026 | Define customer journey, claims eligibility, required evidence, service-level standards, and operational handoffs. | Complete |
| May 31, 2026 | Complete technical design and select integration approach for baggage tracing, case management, notifications, and document storage. | Complete |
| August 1, 2026 | Pilot delayed-baggage claim submission and status tracking in the app for BNA and RDU arrivals. | Complete |
| October 1, 2026 | Expand delayed-baggage claim capability network-wide and begin airport-agent training on digital-case handoffs. | Complete |
| November 15, 2026 | Add damaged-baggage claims, photo upload, and resolution-status notifications in the app. | Planned |
| December 31, 2026 | Review adoption, claim-resolution time, and customer contacts; prioritize 2027 enhancements. | Planned |

**Dependencies and risks**

- Baggage tracing data quality and vendor integration reliability are critical to accurate status updates.
- Claims workflows must account for international baggage rules, liability limits, and evidence requirements.
- Airport teams need a clear process for customers who cannot or do not wish to use digital claims.
- Photo and document storage must meet privacy and retention requirements.

### 6. Support agent tooling

**Description and scope**  
Create a single, role-appropriate guidance experience for customer-support agents that surfaces current policies, decision trees, fare rules, and approved exception guidance. Reduce avoidable supervisor escalations and improve governance of policy content sourced from Confluence and operational updates discussed in Slack.

**Owner**  
Customer Support + Digital

**Priority**  
P2

**Key milestones**

| Target date | Milestone | Status |
|---|---|---|
| April 15, 2026 | Inventory policy content, escalation drivers, and duplicate or conflicting guidance across Confluence, Slack, and support materials. | Complete |
| June 30, 2026 | Define content governance, approval workflow, ownership model, and minimum metadata for policy effective dates and version control. | Complete |
| September 30, 2026 | Launch pilot knowledge experience for refund and disruption policies to a selected customer-support group. | Complete |
| November 30, 2026 | Expand tool coverage to special assistance and baggage claims; add guided decision trees for common exception scenarios. | Planned |
| December 31, 2026 | Measure adoption and escalation reduction; approve 2027 rollout plan for remaining policy domains and airport users. | Planned |

**Dependencies and risks**

- Policy owners must maintain authoritative, dated content and resolve conflicts quickly.
- Integration with existing support tools and identity/access controls may affect rollout timing.
- Tooling will not reduce escalations without aligned delegation limits, coaching, and quality assurance.
- Slack must not remain an unmanaged source of operational policy direction.

## Success Criteria

### Initiative KPIs

| Initiative | Success criteria by December 31, 2026 |
|---|---|
| Refund speed-up | At least 90% of eligible direct-booking refunds issued within 7 calendar days of completed eligibility validation; median issuance time of 5 days or less; refund-status contacts per refund reduced by 25% from the Q1 baseline. |
| Disruption policy refresh | 100% of customer-facing channels use the approved policy language and current effective-date version; at least 90% adherence in sampled Support and airport cases; disruption-policy complaints per 1,000 disrupted passengers reduced by 15% from the Q1 baseline. |
| Baggage claims online | At least 35% of eligible delayed- or damaged-baggage claims initiated digitally after network rollout; 90% of digital claimants receive a status update within 24 hours; baggage-claim contacts per case reduced by 20% from the Q1 baseline. |
| Skyway Miles tier changes | 100% accurate tier calculation and benefit recognition for qualifying members after the September 1 effective date; fewer than 1% of affected members require manual tier correction; benefit utilization and loyalty economics remain within approved plan. |
| Special assistance improvements | At least 95% of booked wheelchair requests have a documented handoff at departure and arrival stations; unaccompanied-minor and pet-in-cabin policy adherence reaches at least 95% in sampled cases; special-assistance complaints per 1,000 relevant trips reduced by 15% from the Q1 baseline. |
| Support agent tooling | At least 75% weekly active use among the pilot and expanded user groups; supervisor escalations for covered policy questions reduced by 20% from the pre-pilot baseline; at least 90% of sampled answers cite current approved guidance. |

### Overall roadmap progress criteria

- All P0 policy, process, training, communications, and control changes are implemented by their stated effective dates without material compliance findings.
- Customer-facing policy changes are published in plain language, with effective dates and transition rules visible before launch.
- Finance, Legal, Policy and Compliance, Digital, Customer Support, and Airport and Ground Operations maintain a single release-readiness review for changes that affect more than one channel.
- Quarterly performance reviews track customer outcomes, operational burden, employee feedback, risk exceptions, and financial impact; corrective actions are assigned within 30 days when targets are missed.
- Q4 peak-travel readiness confirms that policy guidance, customer communications, staffing procedures, and escalation paths are current and usable across channels.
